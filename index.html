About

A short version

I studied Computer Science and Engineering at North South University, and spent nine months as a research assistant at NSU's Design, Inclusion & Access Lab, studying alternative credit-scoring systems for people underserved by traditional banking in Bangladesh — a project that was more about listening carefully than writing code.

Since then I've moved toward data and AI automation: building agent workflows with the Claude API and n8n, doing SQL and Retool work, and occasionally getting pulled into full product builds — a print-template editor, a full website redesign for a Dhaka-based architecture studio.

Outside of that, I read more Stoic, Taoist, and Buddhist writing than is probably useful, and write in Bengali when English starts to feel too clean for what I mean. Both habits show up in how I build: strip a workflow down to what actually matters, then let it run on its own.

How I work

Control what's mine to control

I can't fix a client's messy process by wishing it were different. I can decide what the system I build actually does — so I start there, and let the rest go.

Remove before I add

Most workflows carry steps nobody remembers the reason for. Before I automate anything, I find out what can be cut first.

Build for what changes

Requirements shift, teams change, data gets messier. I'd rather ship something simple that bends than something clever that breaks.

Product design

Foundrio — full website redesign

Foundrio Design & Consultant, an architecture and engineering consultancy in Banani, Dhaka, needed a site that read as considered as their drawings — not another generic construction-firm template.

I ran a full business and UX/UI audit, then built the redesign around an "architectural system" visual language: strong grid and proportion, void-and-mass composition, editorial display type paired with technical labels, and a restrained material palette instead of the usual corporate blue. The new information architecture centers a project-lifecycle hero (concept → design → engineering → construction → completion), services shown as interactive diagrams rather than generic cards, and a numbered project archive.

Full-stack

Cover Generator

Foundrio's design team was recreating engineering-drawing cover sheets by hand for every project. I built a browser-based print template editor instead — drag-and-drop layout, snapping and alignment, reusable templates, print-ready A4/A3 output.

Behind the editor: Supabase for data, Google OAuth with an allowlist-based access model, signed URLs and row-level security policies, and a security pass that closed a handful of XSS gaps. I'm currently rebuilding the canvas layer in Konva.js, after testing a DOM+CSS rendering approach as an alternative.
