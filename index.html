The short version

I studied Computer Science and Engineering at North South University, then spent nine months as a research assistant at NSU's Design, Inclusion & Access Lab — studying alternative credit-scoring systems for people underserved by traditional banking in Bangladesh. More listening than coding.

These days it's mostly data and AI automation: building agent workflows with the Claude API and n8n, doing SQL and Retool work, occasionally getting pulled into full product builds — a print-template editor, a full website redesign for a Dhaka-based architecture studio.

Off-screen, I read more Stoic and Taoist philosophy than is strictly useful, and write in Bengali when English feels too tidy for what I mean. Same instinct either way: cut what doesn't matter, keep what does.

02

How I Work

Control what's mine

Can't fix a client's messy process by wishing it were different. I decide what the system I build actually does — start there, let the rest go.

Remove before I add

Every workflow carries steps nobody remembers the reason for. Before I automate anything, I find out what can just be cut.

Build for what changes

Requirements shift, teams change, data gets messier. Simple and bendy beats clever and brittle, every time.

Foundrio — Full Website Redesign

Foundrio Design & Consultant, an architecture and engineering consultancy in Banani, Dhaka, needed a site that read as considered as their drawings — not another generic construction-firm template.

Full business + UX/UI audit, then a redesign built around an "architectural system" visual language: strong grid and proportion, void-and-mass composition, editorial type paired with technical labels, restrained material palette instead of corporate blue. New IA, project-lifecycle hero, services as interactive diagrams, a numbered project archive.

FULL-STACK

Cover Generator

Foundrio's design team was recreating engineering-drawing cover sheets by hand for every project. I built a browser-based print template editor instead — drag-and-drop layout, snapping and alignment, reusable templates, print-ready A4/A3 output.

Under the hood: Supabase for data, Google OAuth with an allowlist model, signed URLs and row-level security policies, and a security pass that closed a handful of XSS gaps. Currently rebuilding the canvas layer in Konva.js after testing a DOM+CSS approach as an alternative.
